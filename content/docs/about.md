My username "evgnomon" is ευγνώμων in greek, meaning "grateful", which is also what my given name means in persian (فارسی). I was born in Tehran, Iran, to an electrical power engineer father, and grew up in a neighborhood in the south of the city. I learned persian by reading literature a thousand years old, and later learned English so I could write and read code, which is how I have spent most of my working life. I am married with two kids, born in and living in Sweden. I write code as a software engineer, and the story has just started!

I run my own company, Clusterlean AB, through which I work as a senior software engineer and architect across IoT, cloud infrastructure, and large-scale data/ML pipelines, building backend systems and platform tooling for clients such as IKEA, Apple, and Meta. Over the years I have built APIs handling billions of daily requests, led cloud migrations, and designed event-driven and distributed systems.

I hold a Master's degree in Software Engineering with a focus on data and network security from Sharif University of Technology, and a Bachelor's degree in the same field from Iran University of Science and Technology (IUST).

On this website I write essays on technology, government, and belief, working through ideas on power, code, money, and what it means to make versus to take. Alongside the essays I maintain licenses and technical notes, such as the HGL General License and my OpenPGP key, that reflect the same principles I write about applied to how I share and protect my own work.

I also publish a few licenses and manifestos of my own here. The HGL General License is a copyleft license I wrote for my software and other creative work, requiring derivatives to stay open and under the same terms while still allowing private, closed-source use. The BTC Transaction Code is a short manifesto framing Bitcoin as the settlement layer for a work-for-work economy, where value is only recognized when it is acknowledged through a transaction. The ISLAM Sovereign Liberal Autonomy Manifesto is a personal essay on identity, authorship, and autonomy, using "ISLAM" as an acronym to argue for who gets to speak first and set the terms of a conversation or a system.
