title: "Open or Not"
date: 2026-09-27T10:00:00+02:00

A downloadable product and a SaaS are two different markets, and a license acts on them differently.

With SaaS, the code never leaves the provider. Users get a result over the network, not a copy. Someone can take open code, change it, run it for paying users and never share a line, because nothing was distributed. Licenses that act on distribution have nothing to act on here. That is why the AGPL exists.

With a downloadable product, the code itself is what reaches the user. Every copy is a distribution. Whoever wants to sell or ship your work has to hand it over, so the license acts on every copy, every time. There is no hidden server to keep a fork behind.

[HGL](https://evgnomon.org/docs/hgl/) is built for that market. It permanently grants every user the freedom to copy and modify. You may even keep your changes closed, as long as you don't distribute them. The moment you do distribute, the whole source must be available for every user to get a free copy, under HGL, with no additional restriction. What ships stays open, and whoever receives it gets the same rights you got. HGL does not restrict users. It restricts one act: passing the work on with fewer rights than you received.

So when the market is not SaaS, HGL is enough. The product travels as code, the code travels with its license, and the license travels with its rights. No fork can close what it has to hand over. And you can still make money on support, warranties and services around it.

Which brings the real question. If you want to restrict users, no running it here, no changing that, pay per seat, ship it without the source, then why think about open source at all? Open source is not a marketing label or a pricing model. It is the decision to give users the same rights you have. If you hold back one of those rights, you hold back control, and a user who is not in control is a customer, not a partner. Choose closed, and be honest about it, or choose open, and give everything. Anything in between is a closed product wearing an open name.
