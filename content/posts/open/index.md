title: "Open or Not"
date: 2026-09-27T10:00:00+02:00

A downloadable product and a SaaS are two different markets, and a license acts on them differently.

With SaaS, the code never leaves the provider. Users get a result over the network, not a copy. Someone can take open code, change it, run it for paying users and never share a line, because nothing was distributed. Licenses that act on distribution have nothing to act on here. That is why the AGPL exists.

With a downloadable product, the code itself is what reaches the user. Every copy is a distribution. Whoever wants to sell or ship your work has to hand it over, so the license acts on every copy, every time. There is no hidden server to keep a fork behind.

[HGL](https://evgnomon.org/docs/hgl/) is built for that market. It gives you every right the author has: use, study, modify, run, share, sell. It holds back one right only: redistributing with a modified license. Whatever you ship stays HGL, and whoever receives it gets the same rights you got. It does not restrict the user. It restricts one act: taking those rights away from the next user. Someone who wants the code without that identity cannot hold the code at all.

So when the market is not SaaS, HGL is enough. The product travels as code, the code travels with its license, and the license travels with its rights. No fork can close what it has to hand over.

Which brings the real question. If you want to restrict users, no running it here, no changing that, pay per seat, keep your changes to yourself, then why think about open source at all? Open source is not a marketing label or a pricing model. It is the decision to give users the same rights you have. If you hold back one of those rights, you hold back control, and a user who is not in control is a customer, not a partner. Choose closed, and be honest about it, or choose open, and give everything. Anything in between is a closed product wearing an open name.
