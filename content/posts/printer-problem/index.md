*A response to [Losing the Bottleneck: Why AI Threatens More Than Our Jobs](/posts/losing-the-bottleneck/).*

The essay ends on the right question: what gives ordinary people a claim on the economy once their labor no longer does? Its answer is that value gathers where things are scarce, and that AI is removing human labor from the list of scarce things. If that is true, the practical question for anyone who still has a paycheck is simple: what do I turn my labor into, while it still pays, so that the value survives?

This article argues that there is one problem Bitcoin solves permanently, and that it is exactly the problem the essay describes.

## The printer

Call it the printer: AI running on electronic circuits, powered by solar panels, connected by satellite internet. It doesn't need anyone's permission, it doesn't sleep, and every year it can make more of more things.

Anything the printer can make becomes abundant, and anything abundant loses its value. That is what is happening to labor. It is also what will happen to most of the things people save in:

- **Goods and companies.** A car company or a phone maker is a recipe the printer can repeat. "Robot company 2.0," as the essay puts it, is just another set of robots.
- **Energy.** Solar panels plus robots are exactly how energy becomes cheap and plentiful.
- **Chips and minerals.** Factories and mines can be built by machines.
- **Gold.** Robots can dig more of it, and eventually mine asteroids.
- **Skills.** Every prompt and correction teaches the printer your job.

Saving in any of these means saving in something the printer will eventually dilute.

## Two ways savings die

A store of value can fail in two ways:

1. **Dilution.** Someone makes more of it, and your share shrinks. In the AI economy, the printer does this.
2. **Seizure.** Someone takes it from you. Anything physical, registered or held by a third party can be confiscated, frozen or taxed away.

So the test is simple: can the printer make more of it, and can anyone take it?

## The test

| Asset | Printer can't make more | Can't be seized | Passes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bitcoin, self-custody | Yes | Yes | **Yes** |
| Bitcoin on an exchange | Yes | No | No |
| Land and property | Yes | No | No |
| Gold | No | No | No |
| Stocks, funds, companies | No | No | No |
| Cash and bank deposits | No | No | No |
| Skills and knowledge | No | Yes | No |

Land comes closest. No machine makes more of it, but it sits in one place, it is listed in a government register and it cannot be moved or hidden. Gold fails both parts: robots can mine it, and governments have confiscated it before, as the United States did in 1933.

## Why Bitcoin passes

**The printer can't make more.** Bitcoin's supply follows a fixed schedule written into the protocol: new issuance halves roughly every four years and stops at 21 million coins. The key part is the difficulty adjustment. About every two weeks, the network adjusts how hard mining is, so that blocks keep arriving at the same pace. If the printer runs a million times more mining machines on cheap solar power, mining simply gets a million times harder. More production power produces no more supply. Bitcoin is almost the only asset designed so that effort can't create more of it.

**Nobody can take it, if you hold the keys.** Bitcoin in your own wallet is controlled by a secret only you know. It isn't in a register, a vault or a bank. It can't be frozen without your cooperation, and it can cross a border in your memory. The protection has one condition: self-custody. The United States government holds roughly 328,000 BTC, and almost all of it was seized. Most of it came from coins people left on exchanges, mishandled keys, or had to hand over after arrest. Bitcoin on an exchange is as seizable as a bank account.

**Anyone can verify it.** The total supply and every transaction are public. You don't have to trust a central bank, a company or an auditor to know that your share hasn't been diluted.

## Turning labor into stored time

A person who is about to be replaced still has one scarce thing: their time. But time can't be stored, and once the economy no longer needs it, it has no market value. The essay puts it well: what the company books as efficiency, the worker lives as unemployment.

Bitcoin offers a way out of that trap at the personal level. Hours of labor that still pay today can be converted into something that the printer can't dilute and no one can easily take. It is a way to carry the value of your paid time into a future where that time may no longer pay.

## The problem it solves permanently

Bitcoin doesn't solve every problem. It solves one, permanently:

> It lets a person hold a share that no machine can dilute and no authority can easily take.

That problem isn't specific to this generation or to AI. Every era has had its printer: kings clipping coins, governments printing money, empires confiscating land. AI is the most powerful printer yet. A rule written into a protocol, enforced by mathematics and verified by everyone, is the first answer to it that doesn't depend on someone choosing not to print.

## What it leaves open

An honest case should also say what this doesn't cover:

- **Supply is guaranteed, the price is not.** The printer can't make more bitcoin, but what a coin buys depends on people still wanting it. The test above protects against dilution and seizure, not against falling demand.
- **The cap is protected by rules and cryptography.** In theory, the network could agree to change its rules, or future quantum computers could threaten older signatures before an upgrade. Both are considered unlikely, but neither is ruled out by physics.
- **Keys can be lost or forced.** Self-custody removes the third party, and with it the safety net.
- **It is a personal answer, not a collective one.** Bitcoin protects the people who hold it, and early, large holders the most. The essay's broader question, a claim on the economy for everyone, still runs through politics: who owns the bottlenecks, and how their gains are shared.

## Conclusion

If labor is losing its scarcity, the rational move for anyone still earning is to convert labor into something that stays scarce. By the strict test of this article, which asks whether the printer can make more of it and whether anyone can take it, Bitcoin held in self-custody is the strongest candidate there is. It doesn't promise a price. It promises that your share stays your share.
